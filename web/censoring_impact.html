Censoring Impact Calculator

How much of a prediction model's reported performance depends on treating patients who leave follow-up as non-informatively censored? This page holds your models' predictions fixed, reassigns a chosen share of departed patients as deaths at the moment of departure, and shows how discrimination and calibration move.

Your data stay on this computer. The file is read and analyzed inside this browser page; nothing is uploaded or sent anywhere. The page works offline.

1 · Data

One CSV row per evaluation unit (a patient, or a patient-period such as a yearly landmark). Needed: a patient identifier, follow-up time, whether death was observed (0/1), whether the patient left follow-up (0/1, the same on every row of that patient), and one risk score per model. Optional: each model's predicted risk of death by a horizon (0–1) for calibration, a 0/1 column marking rows whose horizon is fully observable, and a route column (censored, dead or random for departed patients).

2 · Columns

Models

ReferenceNameRisk score (higher = higher risk) Predicted risk by horizon (optional)

3 · Settings

Calibration needs the horizon and at least one predicted-risk column. Censoring weights for the calibration slope are estimated from these data under each scenario.

4 · Results

How it works

  • Nothing is refitted. Only the outcome definition changes: in each scenario a share of departed patients is assumed to have died at the moment of departure, with follow-up time unchanged.
  • Scenarios: as analyzed (departures censored); the shares you choose; the stayer rate (the proportion of never-departed patients with a recorded death); route-informed (if a route column is given); all departed patients died.
  • Random assignments are drawn at the patient level, generated once and shared by every metric. Point estimates in random scenarios are means over assignments.
  • Intervals are 95% percentile intervals from a bootstrap that resamples patients; in random scenarios each replicate uses one stored assignment chosen at random. They are computed for the analysis as conducted, the stayer rate, the route-informed scenario and the extreme.
  • Harrell's C and the Kaplan–Meier and censoring estimators follow scikit-survival. In the scenarios without random assignment, C and O:E match the Python tool exactly; the calibration slope is solved to full convergence and can differ from the Python tool's in the fourth decimal place, and is shown as unavailable (—) when the fit does not converge. Numbers of patients assigned death are rounded as in the Python tool (halves to the even number). Random scenarios use a different random-number generator from the Python tool, so they differ slightly from it (Monte Carlo variation), as do bootstrap intervals.
  • The scenarios are alternative outcome definitions, not a formal bound on true performance.
Method: Fang YW, Huang YC, Wang JT, Tsai MH. How much is the non-informative censoring assumption worth? A fixed-model sensitivity analysis of time-updated mortality prediction in hemodialysis. Code and Python version: github.com/chaosmyth6315/censoring-impact-hemodialysis.
